# Trajectory optimisation

The swing-up trajectory is computed offline by **direct collocation** (`design/rotary_lib.py:swing_up`, CasADi with IPOPT) on the same Lagrangian model, including the motor and its voltage limit. State $x = (q, \dot q)$, input $u$ (volts).

$$\min_{x,u} \int_0^{T_s} u^2 + 0.2\,\dot u^2 + 0.05\,|\dot q_{2:}|^2\, dt$$

subject to

- the Hermite-Simpson collocation constraints $x_{k+1} - x_k = \tfrac{h}{6}\,(f_k + 4 f_c + f_{k+1})$ on every interval;
- hanging at rest at the start, $x(0) = (0, \pi, 0, \dots; 0)$, and **upright at rest** at the end, $x(T_s) = 0$;
- $|u| \le 9$ V (the amplifier limit is 10 V; the 1 V margin is for the feedback) and $|q_1| \le 2$ rad (arm travel).

$T_s = 4$ s. The first guess is the forward simulation of a sinusoidal voltage from hanging; the solve is a continuation in the voltage bound (10 V, then 9 V) followed by two refinements of the grid (80 to 160 to 320 intervals). The refinement matters: the swing-up of the double pendulum is chaotic, and on the coarse grid the collocation error alone made the trajectory untrackable. On the final grid the trajectory is dynamically exact to the accuracy that the time-varying LQR corrects.

The trajectory, its voltage and the gain schedule are written into [Ctrl](block:Ctrl) as the constant table `Tab` (one row every 10 ms, 401 rows: $x^*$, $u^*$ and $K$), generated by `design/build_model.py`.
