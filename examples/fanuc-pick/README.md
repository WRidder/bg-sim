# FANUC pick — Cycle-time optimisation of a FANUC LR-10iA/10 pick and place with a five-zone suction gripper.

A FANUC LR-10iA/10 picks a carton from a conveyor with a five-zone suction gripper and places it on a
table, turned by 90°. Each zone is a pneumatic cylinder that extends a bellows suction cup, with its own
vacuum ejector. The carton has a configurable size, mass and centre of mass. Two variants carry a loose
ball or a loose bottle inside a see-through carton. Four trajectory families are compared, and
`design/optimize.py` finds the shortest cycle time of each that keeps a holding margin and stays inside
the robot's datasheet limits.

**The arm is kinematic.** It follows the planned joint trajectory exactly, as long as the plan stays inside
the LR-10iA/10 datasheet limits: the joint ranges and rates, an acceleration and jerk limit per joint,
the payload, and the allowable wrist moment and inertia. These limits are constraints of the optimiser
and are checked in every run (`Planner/util_*`). There are no arm dynamics, motor or drive models and
no joint control loops. The dynamics are in the gripper, the carton and its contents.

The model docs (Docs tab, `models/*.docs`) hold the derivations and the tables. Their source is
`tools/example_docs/src/fanuc-pick.md`.

## Models

| model | what |
| --- | --- |
| `pick-place` | 1.5 kg carton (300 x 200 x 120 mm), five cups, Cartesian blended motion (family 3) at the default limits |
| `offset-com` | the same carton with the centre of mass 60 mm and 30 mm off its centre, gripped at the box centre |
| `loose-sphere` | a 0.6 kg carton shell with a loose 0.5 kg ball (80 mm) |
| `loose-cylinder` | a 0.6 kg carton shell with a loose 0.5 kg bottle (cylinder 70 x 120 mm) lying in it |
| `too-aggressive` | a trajectory beyond the gripper's holding capacity: the cups peel and the carton drops |
| `static-hold` | validation: the offset-CoM carton carried slowly, so it hangs still in the cruise |
| `pick-place-best`, `offset-com-best`, `loose-sphere-best`, `loose-cylinder-best` | the optimum of `design/optimize.py` for each load case (families 1–4, tool level) |
| `*-tilt-best` | the same families with the waiter tilt: the tool leans with the specific force of the carton (`design/optimize_free.py`, stage A1) |
| `*-opt` | the highest pick rate of `design/optimize_free.py`: free timing (family 5), tilt, yaw window and the gripper timing |

All models share one plant (`design/build_models.py`). They differ only in their global parameters,
and the loose-content models add the content body. In the editor every global is in the Equations
manager, and the dashboard has sliders for the family, the speed and acceleration limits, the jerk
share, the blend distance, the lift height, the cylinder lead, the vacuum wait, the centre of mass and
the pick and place yaw.

## The robot

No URDF, xacro or mesh of the LR-10iA/10 is published (not in `ros-industrial/fanuc`, not in
`fanuc_experimental`, nor elsewhere on GitHub). The geometry is therefore the closest FANUC arm whose
sources are open: the **LR Mate 200iD/7L**, from the same slim, enclosed LR family, with six axes and a
spherical wrist. It has a reach of 911 mm, against 1101 mm for the LR-10iA/10.

- Kinematics and meshes come from `fanuc_lrmate200id_support/urdf/lrmate200id7l_macro.xacro` of
  [ros-industrial/fanuc](https://github.com/ros-industrial/fanuc), branch `noetic-devel` at
  `d8f42bd73584b255df87098395512538882caea1` (package 0.6.0). The license is BSD-3-Clause, and its
  text is in `meshes/LICENSE`. The meshes are converted to GLB (`design/convert_meshes.py`).
- The limits are the LR-10iA/10's (catalogue RLR-10iA(E)-01, 2022.3):
  - joint rates 300, 230, 340, 500, 400 and 800 °/s;
  - payload 10 kg;
  - wrist moment J4/J5/J6 21/21/10 N·m;
  - wrist inertia 0.77/0.77/0.28 kg·m².
- Joint ranges: J1 ±185°, J2 −96..139°, J3 −70..213°, J4 ±190°, J5 ±125°, J6 ±360°.
- Acceleration and jerk limits: no FANUC datasheet gives them. They are **assumed**: each rate limit is
  reached in 0.15 s (J1–J3) or 0.10 s (J4–J6), and each acceleration limit in 0.05 s. Replace them in
  `design/fanuc.py` with the controller's values when known.
- The planner (`library/PickPlanner.jl`, generated by `design/build_library.py` from `design/planner.py`):
  - is a 1 kHz discrete block, the robot's interpolation tick;
  - computes the joint references in closed form (IK of the tool-down pose);
  - computes the gripper pose and its body twist;
  - computes the use of every limit.
- `ToolMotion` imposes the twist on the gripper as a spatial flow source, holding it to first order
  between the ticks.

## The gripper

The gripper is estimated from the reference video (the user's own design). Every dimension is a global
parameter.

| part | value |
| --- | --- |
| layout | five zones: centre, and four at a pitch radius of 40 mm on the gripper axes (`lay_deg` turns them) |
| cylinders | 16 mm bore, stroke 75 mm, 120 N extend and 100 N retract force at 6 bar, free speed 0.5 m/s (meter-out), end stops 2e5 N/m with cushions |
| rods | 60 g moving mass, compliant in their guides (5e4 N/m lateral, 200 N·m/rad bending) |
| cups | 1.5-fold bellows, lip 32 mm, effective area 5 cm², rest length 30 mm, axial 2500 N/m, lateral 1500 N/m, tilt 1 N·m/rad, lip friction 0.5 |
| cup bellows (nonlinear, estimates) | the folds bottom after 6 mm of compression (`k_bot` 2.5e4 N/m, cubic); the vacuum lifts the bellows by `A_fold`·dp/k_ax = 4.1 mm at 60 kPa; lateral and tilt stiffness × (1 + dp/p_atm) × exp(−3·ext/L0), so +59 % under 60 kPa and softer when the cup is pulled long |
| vacuum | one compact ejector per zone, 60 kPa end vacuum, 12 l/min free air; cardboard permeance 4e-7 m/(s·Pa) |
| gripper mass | 1.3 kg (base 0.5 kg, 5 x (cylinder 0.1 kg + rod 0.06 kg)) |

**Why one ejector per zone.** A zone whose cup leaks or peels does not pull the vacuum of the other
zones down with it. A common ejector would see the leak of the worst cup as a load on all of them. The
zones can also be switched off one by one (`zone1`..`zone5`), so a smaller parcel uses only the cups
that land on it. In `too-aggressive` the zones lose their seal one after the other: each zone's
vacuum (`Cup*/dp`) collapses only when its own lip lifts.

**The sequence.**
1. The cylinders extend `t_ext` before the pick contact, during the approach.
2. The vacuum opens `t_vac` before the contact.
3. The robot waits `T_seal` at the pick.
4. It lifts by `H_lift`, transfers and lowers.
5. At the place it switches the vacuum off and vents for `T_rel`.
6. It departs.

`ret_hold = 1` retracts the cylinders while the carton is carried and extends them again before the
place.

**The holding margin.** `U` of a cup (`SuctionCup`) is (axial pull + 4 · bending moment / lip diameter +
shear / µ) / (A_eff · vacuum). 1 means pull-off, peel or slip. The margin of a zone is 1 − max U while
the carton is carried, and `M_tot` is the smallest over the zones (`PickMonitor`). The requirement
`M_req` is 0.5, a safety factor of 2. For the loose contents it is 0.3; see the results below for why.
A drop is the carton moving more than 20 mm from where it sat in the gripper at the lift.

## Trajectory families

| family | path | moves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 joint PTP | lift, transfer and lower as separate moves with stops | synchronised joint moves, jerk-limited S-profile at `kv`, `ka` of the joint limits |
| 2 Cartesian | straight lines with stops | jerk-limited S-profile at `v_c`, `a_c`, `j_c` along the path (yaw `w_c`, `al_c`, `jw_c`) |
| 3 Cartesian blended | straight lines, the next move starts `r_b` before the corner | as 2, overlapped |
| 4 joint blended | joint space, the transfer starts `r_b` before the end of the lift | as 1, overlapped |

`lam` is the jerk phase as a share of each acceleration phase (0 is a trapezoid, 1 a pure S-curve).

## Results

`uv run examples/fanuc-pick/design/optimize.py` (about 25 min on 20 cores). It searches each load case
and family for the shortest cycle time that keeps:
- `M_tot ≥ M_req` in the reduced model, with no drop;
- every robot limit at 100 % or below;
- the carton bottom at least 20 mm above the support away from the pick and the place.

**Decision variables.** A speed scale (the share of the joint limits, or of the Cartesian reference
limits) and the shape of the trajectory:
- the acceleration-to-speed ratio;
- the jerk share;
- the lift height;
- the blend distance;
- the vacuum wait.

**Method.** A grid with coordinate refinement over the shape. For each shape, a bisection finds the
fastest feasible speed scale. The reduced model (`design/reduced.py`, numba-compiled, about 0.5 s a run)
uses the same cup, contact and wall laws as the bg-sim blocks. The results are in `design/results.json`,
and `build_models.py` turns each case's optimum into the model `<case>-best`.

What the optimiser finds:
- **The gripper sets the cycle time, not the robot.** Every optimum uses only 55–68 % of the
  LR-10iA/10 limits. Going faster is possible for the arm (`too-aggressive`: 83 % of the limits) but not
  for the cups.
- **The default trajectory is not safe.** Family 3 at its default limits takes 1.659 s, but its margin
  is −0.05: the most loaded cup goes past its capacity during the transfer. The carton just stays on
  (bg-sim and the reduced model agree). The optimum, family 4 (joint blended), takes 1.612 s with a margin
  of 0.50: 3 % faster and safe.
- **Blending is what pays.** The families that stop at the corners take 1.98 s (joint PTP) and 2.04 s
  (Cartesian). Blending cuts 0.36–0.43 s. For the solid carton the joint blend is fastest (1.612 s against
  1.625 s for the Cartesian blend). For the loose contents the Cartesian blend is fastest (by 0.01–0.12 s).
- **No vacuum wait.** Every optimum has `T_seal` = 0: the vacuum is ready within the time the
  cylinders and the approach take. The Cartesian optima lift 30–50 mm; the joint-blended optima lift
  50–70 mm with an 80 mm blend, so the transfer starts while the carton still rises.
- **Grip over the centre of mass.** With the CoM 60/30 mm off centre, gripping over the CoM costs only
  1 % (1.626 s). Gripping at the box centre is infeasible at any speed: at 5 % speed the margin is 0.16.
- **Loose contents.** The 1.1 kg load with M_req 0.3 can go a little faster (1.51 s with the ball, 1.49 s
  with the bottle). The content's impacts on the walls set the margin.
- **The nonlinear bellows cost 2–10 %.** With the linear cups of the first version the optima were
  1.578 / 1.597 / 1.392 / 1.359 s. The bellows laws (vacuum lift stroke, vacuum- and stroke-dependent
  side stiffness) change how the carton swings on the cups, and the peaks of the most loaded cup rise.
  The loose contents lose most (8–10 %): the default trajectory goes from M +0.09 / +0.05 to −0.20 / −0.11.

## Free optimisation: the highest pick rate

`uv run examples/fanuc-pick/design/optimize_free.py [--refine] [--loose-strict]` (about 75 min per pass on
20 cores) drops the fixed families and optimises every freedom the cell has, for the highest **pick rate**:
picks per minute = 60 / (T_cyc + T_ret), where T_ret is the empty joint move from the depart end back to
the approach start at the robot's full rates. The constraints are those of `optimize.py` (margin, no drop,
every LR-10iA/10 limit at 100 % or below, the carton corners clear of the support, now with the tilted
carton) plus a vent condition: the residual holding force at the depart start is at most 20 % of the
parcel weight.

The freedoms are added one at a time, each stage warm-started from the one before (CMA-ES on the reduced
model, then a push along the stage's own time scale to the constraint boundary; two passes, the second
restarting every stage from the first pass's optimum):

| stage | freedoms |
| --- | --- |
| A0 | the best of families 1–4 (`optimize.py`) |
| A1 | families 3 and 4 with the **waiter tilt**: the tool axis leans towards the specific force g − a of the carton (`tilt_gain`, largest tilt `tilt_max_deg`, filter `T_tw`, fade at the pick and the place `T_tr`); level at both contacts |
| A2 | **family 5, free timing**: own durations for lift, transfer and lower, the transfer starts and ends anywhere inside the carry (lift and lower overlap it), asymmetric acceleration and deceleration shares, with the tilt |
| A3 | + the **yaw turn spread** over its own window of the carry |
| A4 | + the **gripper timing**: vacuum on before the contact, seal wait, vent time, approach and depart durations |

Picks per minute (T_cyc + T_ret, holding margin, peak tool tilt, robot limit use); "no gain" means the
stage did not beat the best so far:

What the free optimisation finds:
- **15 % more picks for the solid carton**: 28.3 → 32.6 picks/min (box), 28.7 → 32.4 (offset CoM), at the
  same margin of 0.5. The loose contents gain 11 % (ball, 30.5 → 33.9) and 16 % (bottle, 30.1 → 34.9).
- **The waiter tilt pays within the fixed families** (A1: +1.0 to +2.8 picks/min; 1–7° of tilt, gain
  0.05–0.5). With free timing (A2–A4) the optimiser shapes the transfer itself (asymmetric
  acceleration and deceleration, the transfer overlapping the lift and the lower), and the tilt drops to
  0–1° for the solid carton: the smooth profile already keeps the shear low. The loose ball keeps 7°.
- **The gripper timing adds as much again** (A4: +1.2 to +2.0 picks/min). The vacuum opens
  0.02–0.17 s before the contact so the seal wait is zero, the vent takes 10–76 ms instead of 80 ms (the
  cup chamber vents in about 0.4 ms), and the approach and depart run in 0.18–0.20 s instead of 0.24–0.26 s.
- **Spreading the yaw turn** adds little (0–0.4 picks/min): the 90° turn is not what loads the cups.
- **The robot becomes the limit.** The fixed-family optima use 55–68 % of the LR-10iA/10 limits; the free
  optima 94–100 %. For the bottle the robot limit is active and the margin (0.34) is above the required
  0.3.
- **Loose contents at a margin of 0.5** (`--loose-strict`): reachable with the tilt and free timing, at
  27.5 picks/min (ball, 3° tilt) and 28.5 picks/min (bottle, 9° tilt). The fixed-family optima for 0.3 (A0)
  do not hold 0.5; families 3/4 with the tilt (A1) do, at 25.4 and 26.5 picks/min.
- The models `<case>-tilt-best` (stage A1) and `<case>-opt` (the best stage) carry these optima; the
  `-best` models keep the fixed-family optima. CMA-ES is a stochastic local search: the numbers are good
  optima, not proven global ones (the second pass still gained up to 4 % in some stages).

## Notes and limitations

- **Arm.** The geometry is that of the LR Mate 200iD/7L. The arm is kinematic, and the LR-10iA/10's
  acceleration and jerk limits are assumed (see above).
- **Cups.** The cups are lumped `SuctionCup` elements with no air dynamics in the hoses. The cylinders
  have no air dynamics either: the valve sets the force at once and a meter-out damper sets the speed.
- **Reduced model.** It holds the rods rigid at their extended stop, while bg-sim keeps them compliant.
  The difference in the margin is at most a few hundredths (see the validation).
- **Solver.** The models use Rodas5P. FBDF stops when the cup lips land and their gap falls through
  the `lost` latch level (see the user manual, vacuum gripping).
