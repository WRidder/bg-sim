# X5.8 brief: Wopereis et al. 2018, multimodal aerial locomotion with a contact controller

Source: H. W. Wopereis, W. W. van de Ridder, T. J. W. Lankhorst, L. Klooster, E. M. Bukai,
D. Wuthier, G. Nikolakopoulos, S. Stramigioli, J. B. C. Engelen, M. Fumagalli, "Multimodal Aerial
Locomotion: An Approach to Active Tool Handling", IEEE Robotics & Automation Magazine, Dec. 2018.
The orchestrator extracted the facts below from the paper; the PDF itself is not in the repo.

## System

- **Multirotor (aerial locomotion).** The paper uses a hexarotor: 80 cm diameter without
  propellers, 2.1 kg, PX4 on a Pixhawk 2.1, 950 Kv motors with 10x4.5 in propellers, 16 V tethered
  supply, maximum total thrust F_u = 78 N. It uses long arms so the manipulator passes between
  the front propellers.
- **Manipulator.** One actuated DoF: a servo (Dynamixel MX-106R) rotating about y_b, plus a carbon
  tube 0.60 m long (OD 12 mm, ID 10.5 mm, treated as rigid). The manipulator weighs 0.23 kg.
  - The constant distance from Σb to Σe is L_m.
  - The manipulator angle μ is the angle between x_b and p_be, positive counter-clockwise about y_b.
  - The servo is assumed fast enough to track its setpoint.
- **Flexible joint.** An M5 rubber shock mount connects the arm tip to the end-effector. It acts as
  a rotational compliance, a spherical joint with stiffness and damping, so the end-effector's
  orientation is decoupled from the arm's.
- **End-effector (ground locomotion).** Mass 0.15 kg. Three actuated omnidirectional wheels sit on
  the base platform at 120°. Each wheel is driven in one direction and has free rollers in the
  other, so the end-effector's planar pose on the surface is fully controllable given friction.
  - Open-loop Cartesian body-velocity commands in [-1, 1].
  - A brush is suspended on three springs and gives a constant 6 N once compressed. Modelling the
    brush is optional.
- **Wall.** Vertical, aligned with the (y_w, z_w) plane, at x_w = 1.6 m. x_e is perpendicular to the
  wall and points into it. In contact, Σe has the same orientation as Σw.

## Frames

- Σw is the world frame, z up.
- Σb is the multirotor body frame: origin at the CoM, x_b forward, z_b along the thrust.
- Σe is the end-effector frame: oriented as Σw, origin at the elastic joint.
- Roll, pitch and yaw of the multirotor are (φ_b, θ_b, ψ_b).
- The origin of Σe always lies in the (x_b, z_b) plane. The pitch angle and the thrust therefore
  set the contact force.

## Phases (switching control strategy, paper Fig. 5)

1. **Free flight.** The free-flight controller tracks manual position setpoints
   p_sp = (x_sp, y_sp, z_sp) and ψ_sp. The manipulator setpoint is μ_sp = θ_b, which keeps the arm
   level in the world and the end-effector in front.
2. **Engage.** Starts only when the end-effector is within 30 cm of the surface.
   - The contact controller of the earlier paper [2] runs with the three body angles
     (φ_sp, θ_sp, ψ_sp) as references and the height setpoint z_sp = z_b(t_e).
   - μ_sp = θ_b.
   - θ_sp ramps from 12° to 20° over 4 s to soften the impact.
3. **Contact.** The modified, purely angular contact controller below runs. It is position
   independent, so the multirotor follows the end-effector's ground locomotion without any change
   to its setpoints.
   - μ_sp = μ_c, which follows from the equilibrium.
   - θ_sp ramps further, to 27° in the automated runs (25° to 40° in the manual runs).
4. **Disengage.** The free-flight controller runs with the fixed setpoint
   p_sp = (x_b(t_d) − C, y_b(t_d), z_b(t_d)), C = 0.75 m. μ_sp = θ_b.

## Free-flight controller

A PX4-style cascade:
- P on position, then P on velocity, then P on attitude;
- PID on the angular rate (the extra I and D improve rate tracking);
- gravity compensation;
- a static CoM-correction torque about y_b for the arm and end-effector mass, a constant tuned so
  the free-flight setpoint and position match.

The paper notes no integral action in altitude, which leaves a small steady-state height error.
An integrator is allowed in our model, but say which choice you made.

## Contact controller (contact phase)

The end-effector is held fixed on the wall by friction and the normal force. The arm attaches to
the end-effector through a spherical joint at Σe.

- **Dynamics.** Expressed in Σb with screw theory, they are
  `I_b dT/dt = ad^T_T (I_b T) + Ad^T_{H_g^b} W_g + W_b + Ad^T_{H_e^b} W_e`.
  The quasi-static reaction wrench follows from the force balance.
- **Equilibrium.**
  - φ = ψ = 0, θ = θ_sp, all rates 0, μ = μ_c.
  - Thrust F_eq = m g / cos θ_sp, torques zero.
  - Normal force on the wall F_N = m g tan θ_sp.
- **State and input.**
  - State X = [φ, φ̇, θ, θ̇, ψ, ψ̇]^T.
  - Input U = [τ_x, F_u, τ_z, τ_y] in the K-row ordering below, which is a reconstruction. The
    paper's symbols are garbled; the structure is what matters.
- **Control law.**
  - Linearize: Ẋ = A(θ_sp)(X − X_eq) + B(θ_sp)(U − U_eq).
  - Use LQR with gain scheduling over θ_sp: U = −K(θ_sp)(X − X_sp) + U_eq.
- **Gain structure.** Roll and yaw are coupled, and both are stabilized by the roll and yaw
  torques: K has non-zero φ, φ̇, ψ, ψ̇ columns only in the τ_x and τ_z rows. Pitch is stabilized
  by the pitch torque and the thrust variation: θ, θ̇ columns only in the τ_y and F_u rows.
  Everything else is zero.
- **Reported performance.** In the contact phase, |φ_b| and |ψ_b| stayed below 7.5°.

## Experiments to reproduce, as the simulated scenario

- Lift-off at t = 10 s in the paper. In ours, start shortly after t = 0 or in hover.
- Ascend to about 1 m and approach the wall in free flight with incremental position setpoints.
- Engage, with θ_sp ramping 12° → 20° over 4 s.
- Contact, with θ_sp ramping to 27°.
- Up/down ground locomotion: velocity command 0.125 for 6 s, a 2 s pause, then −0.125 for 6 s.
- Disengage: back off C = 0.75 m from the wall and hover.

One automated run lasted 64 s in the paper. A shorter compressed timeline is fine.
