## Common requirements

### Plant
- **Proper multibond bond graphs**, not equation blocks:
  - **Bodies:** `RigidBody2D` or `RigidBody3D` with a mass and an inertia about the COM. The links
    are rods with a distributed mass and the COM at the rod middle, unless the reference hardware
    says otherwise.
  - **Joints:** rigid revolute joints (`Revolute2DRigid` / `Revolute3D`), or the compliant variants
    with a justification. Small viscous joint friction is allowed.
  - **Bases:** a prismatic joint for the cart; a revolute arm for the rotary base.
  - **Gravity:** `GravityPlanar` / gravity in the body.
- **Study first:**
  - `examples/robot-arm-2d/`, `examples/multibond-pendulum-2d/` and `examples/reaction-wheel-2d/`
    (planar);
  - `examples/robot-arm-3d/` and `examples/cube-3d/` (spatial);
  - `examples/inverted-pendulum-cart/` for the existing cart example's scenario and its LQR block
    style;
  - `plan/MULTIBOND_PLAN.md`;
  - `julia/BGSim/NOTES.md`.
- **Actuator:** a single input on the base or first joint, through a real drive:
  - a DC motor (`stdlib:drives/ElectricDrive` or `converters/DCMotor`) with a gearbox or belt and
    a voltage saturation;
  - the rotary base uses SRV02-like parameters: gear ratio, k_t, R_m, efficiency.
  - The other joints are passive.
- **Parameters:** physically plausible and traceable.
  - The rotary examples use the Quanser rotary double inverted pendulum. Use its published
    specification if you can read it (the URL above or the Quanser datasheet).
  - Otherwise state clearly which values are assumed.
  - The triple variants extend the double with a third link of similar construction.

### Scenario (identical structure for all six)
1. **t = 0 to t_on (≈ 1 s):** hanging straight down, at rest, controller output 0 (motor voltage
   0). Nothing moves; this is a check.
2. **t_on:** swing-up starts.
   - Use a feed-forward trajectory from offline **trajectory optimization**: direct collocation in
     `design/design.py` with `uv run`, e.g. CasADi + IPOPT through inline script metadata, on the
     same model as the bond graph, including the motor and its voltage limit. The trajectory goes
     from all links down to all links up in a fixed time T_s.
   - Track it with **time-varying LQR** (TVLQR, from the linearization along the trajectory).
   - Energy-based swing-up is acceptable for the double pendulums only if it is reliable; the
     triple needs the optimized trajectory.
3. **Catch:** near the upright, switch to an infinite-horizon **LQR** balance controller.
   - Use a discrete controller, e.g. 1 kHz with `@discrete`, like the existing LQR blocks.
   - Use a clear switching rule and record the phase signal.
4. **Disturbance:** once stable for ≥ 3 s, apply a short impulse to the tip link, e.g. a force
   pulse through `ExternalWrenchPlanar` / `ExternalWrenchSpatial` or an equivalent bond-graph
   source.
   - It must be large enough to be visible: tip deflection ≥ ~5–10° for the double, smaller for
     the triple if needed.
   - The balance controller must recover it.
5. Run until ~3 s after the recovery.

### Design, checks and verification
- **Design-model check:** `design.py` must prove its model matches the bond graph. Compare an
  open-loop free swing from a non-trivial initial condition, design ODE vs the bond-graph run.
  Report the max angle difference.
- **Gains:** controller gains, the trajectory and the TVLQR gain schedule are generated into the
  example's `library/*.jl` controller block as constant tables. This makes the model document
  reproducible from `design/design.py`; a `build_model.py` generator is fine.
- **`expected.json` checks** (with justified tolerances):
  - at rest before t_on;
  - upright reached by t_on + T_s + margin;
  - all link angles within a small band of upright before the disturbance;
  - peak deflection after the disturbance above a minimum, so the disturbance is real;
  - recovered within a band by the end;
  - motor voltage within its saturation;
  - cart travel within the rail limit, where there is one;
  - no W206/E-diagnostics;
  - `scene_bindings`.

### 3D scene, docs and e2e
- **3D scene:** `scenes/<example>.sdf` with joint bindings for every joint (rail or arm, links),
  links visibly different, a base or rail, and zero binding diagnostics.
- **Docs:** model docs in the W5.6 format: `models/<model>.docs/docs.json`, `pages/*.md`,
  `assets/<12-hex sha256>.<ext>`, schema `contracts/schemas/docs.schema.json`; see `web/src/docs/`
  for the supported Markdown. Pages:
  - overview;
  - the multibond structure;
  - equations of motion (KaTeX);
  - trajectory optimization formulation;
  - TVLQR and LQR;
  - results plots: PNG from real runs, content-hash named;
  - `block:` and `signal:` links that resolve.
- **e2e:** one real-worker spec per WP covering both examples.
  - It runs each, plots a link angle and checks upright before the disturbance and recovery at
    the end, then opens 3D with valid bindings and checks a joint at two times.
  - Pattern: `web/tests/e2e/reaction-wheel-1d.spec.ts`.
  - Additive DEC-M5-6 edits to `web/playwright.config.ts` and `tools/check.py`; re-read them
    right before editing, because other agents edit them too.
