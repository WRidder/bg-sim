# FANUC LR-10iA/10 pick and place with a five-zone suction gripper

A FANUC LR-10iA/10 picks a 1.5 kg carton (300 x 200 x 120 mm) from a conveyor and places it on a table, turned by 90°. Its gripper has five zones. Each zone is a pneumatic cylinder that extends a bellows suction cup, with its own vacuum ejector. The question is which trajectory gives the shortest cycle time that still holds the carton with a margin.

**The arm is kinematic.** It follows the planned joint trajectory exactly, as long as the plan stays inside the LR-10iA/10 datasheet limits: the joint ranges and rates, an acceleration and jerk limit per joint, the payload, and the allowable wrist moment and inertia. These limits are checked in every run as `Planner/util_*` (in % of the limit) and are constraints of the optimiser. There are no arm dynamics, drives or joint servos. The dynamics are in the gripper (cylinders, compliant rods, bellows cups, vacuum), the carton and its contents.

![The model canvas|900](@fig:diagram)

The signal chain:

```
PickPlanner (1 kHz) --V, Vd, tk--> ToolMotion (spatial flow source) ==> gripper junction
     |  qr (joint references, to the 3D view)          ==> per zone: rod joint (Prismatic3DLimit, PneumaticCylinder)
     |  ue1..5 (cylinder valves), uv1..5 (vacuum)       ==> rod body ==> SuctionCup <== VacuumGenerator
     '--gref, held, act--> PickMonitor <-- U1..5, Pose                ==> carton (RigidBody3D on 4 ContactPoints)
```

- [Planner](block:Planner) is generated by `design/build_library.py` from `design/planner.py`. Every 1 ms tick it computes the joint references in closed form, the gripper pose `gref`, its body twist `V` and twist rate `Vd`, the valve signals and the use of every robot limit.
- [Tool](block:Tool) imposes the twist on the gripper, holding it to first order between the ticks (`V + Vd (t - tk)`), so the gripper moves smoothly.
- Each zone `k` has the following parts:
  - a fixed mount `JM<k>`;
  - the rod joint [Rod1](block:Rod1), a `Prismatic3DLimit` with the stroke as its end stops and compliant in its guide;
  - the cylinder [Cyl1](block:Cyl1) on the joint's drive port;
  - the rod body `RodBody<k>`;
  - the cup [Cup1](block:Cup1);
  - its ejector [Gen1](block:Gen1).
- The carton [Parcel](block:Parcel) rests on four `ContactPoint` corners on the support plane.
- [Monitor](block:Monitor) keeps the peak `U` of every zone while the carton is carried, the margins and the drop latch.

The run: about 2 s with `Rodas5P`, `reltol` 1e-6, `abstol` 1e-8, 500 output points per second.

## What to look at

- the cup utilisation [Cup1/U](signal:Cup1/U) to [Cup5/U](signal:Cup5/U) (1 = pull-off, peel or slip) and the holding margin [Monitor/M_tot](signal:Monitor/M_tot);
- the vacuum [Cup1/dp](signal:Cup1/dp) building up from the lip contact;
- the strokes [Rod1/qout](signal:Rod1/qout): the cylinders run out during the approach;
- the robot limits [Planner/util_max](signal:Planner/util_max) and the wrist load [Planner/util_M](signal:Planner/util_M);
- the drop detection [Monitor/sep](signal:Monitor/sep) and [Monitor/held_ok](signal:Monitor/held_ok).

# Robot and trajectories

## Geometry and limits

No URDF, xacro or mesh of the LR-10iA/10 is published. The geometry is the closest FANUC arm whose sources are open, the **LR Mate 200iD/7L**: the same slim LR family, six axes, a spherical wrist and a reach of 911 mm (the LR-10iA/10 has 1101 mm). The kinematics and meshes come from `fanuc_lrmate200id_support/urdf/lrmate200id7l_macro.xacro` of github.com/ros-industrial/fanuc, branch `noetic-devel` at `d8f42bd73584b255df87098395512538882caea1` (package 0.6.0, BSD-3-Clause, see `meshes/LICENSE`).

The limits are those of the **LR-10iA/10** (catalogue RLR-10iA(E)-01):

| joint | range [°] | rate [°/s] | acceleration [°/s²] (assumed) | jerk [°/s³] (assumed) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| J1 | ±185 | 300 | 2000 | 40 000 |
| J2 | −96..139 | 230 | 1533 | 30 667 |
| J3 | −70..213 | 340 | 2267 | 45 333 |
| J4 | ±190 | 500 | 5000 | 100 000 |
| J5 | ±125 | 400 | 4000 | 80 000 |
| J6 | ±360 | 800 | 8000 | 160 000 |

Payload: 10 kg. Allowable wrist moment J4/J5/J6: 21/21/10 N·m. Allowable wrist inertia: 0.77/0.77/0.28 kg·m².

No FANUC document gives acceleration or jerk limits. They are **assumed**: each rate limit is reached in 0.15 s (J1–J3) or 0.10 s (J4–J6), and each acceleration limit in 0.05 s. Replace them in `design/fanuc.py` with the controller's values.

The wrist load is computed from the following, as point masses at their centres:
- the gripper (1.3 kg, its centre 121 mm below the flange);
- the carton (with its centre of mass);
- the accelerations of the plan, gravity included.

The moment is taken about the wrist centre.

## Kinematics

The tool points straight down, so the inverse kinematics is closed form:

$$q_1 = \operatorname{atan2}(y, x), \qquad r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} - a_1, \qquad z' = z + d_6 - d_1,$$

with the elbow-up solution of the planar two-link problem for $q_2, q_3$, then $q_4 = 0$, $q_5$ keeping the tool vertical, and $q_6 = \psi - q_1$ for the gripper yaw $\psi$. The block evaluates this IK at the tick before, at and after each tick. From the three it forms the joint rates and accelerations, the body twist of the gripper and its rate (`V`, `Vd`). The URDF chain (`design/fanuc.py`) agrees with the closed-form pose to 3e-16 m (`reference.py`), and the `scene_bindings` check confirms it in every run.

## The cycle

The cycle runs from the approach start to the depart end:

1. Approach: from `h_app` = 60 mm above the pick contact pose.
2. The cylinders extend `t_ext` before the contact, and the vacuum opens `t_vac` before it.
3. Wait `T_seal` at the pick.
4. Lift by `H_lift`, transfer (yaw turned by 90°), lower.
5. Vacuum off, vent `T_rel` = 80 ms.
6. Depart 60 mm up.

The cylinders stay out while the carton is carried (`ret_hold = 0`).

## Families

| family | path | profile |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 joint PTP | lift, transfer and lower are separate moves with stops | synchronised joint moves: each joint's time from `kv`, `ka` x its limits; the slowest joint sets the time |
| 2 Cartesian | straight lines with stops | along the path at `v_c`, `a_c`, `j_c`; the yaw at `w_c`, `al_c`, `jw_c`, synchronised |
| 3 Cartesian blended | straight lines; each move starts `r_b` before the end of the one before | as 2, overlapped (a rounded corner) |
| 4 joint blended | joint space; the transfer starts `r_b` before the end of the lift | as 1, overlapped |

Every move is a jerk-limited S-profile. `lam` is the jerk phase as a share of the acceleration phase: 0 is a trapezoid, 1 a pure S-curve. For a move of length $L$, the time is the largest of the velocity, acceleration and jerk bounds:

$$T = \max\left(T_0,\ \left(J\,L / j_{max}\right)^{1/3}\right), \qquad J = \frac{1}{(1-\alpha)(\alpha - t_j)\,t_j},$$

with $T_0$ the time of the velocity and acceleration limits, $\alpha$ the acceleration share and $t_j$ the jerk share of the profile.

# Gripper, cups and vacuum

## The five zones

The gripper is estimated from the reference video. Every dimension is a global parameter.

| part | value | global |
| --- | --- | --- |
| zone layout | centre, and four at a pitch radius of 40 mm on the gripper axes | `r_p`, `lay_deg` |
| cylinder | 16 mm bore, stroke 75 mm, 120 N extend and 100 N retract force, 0.5 m/s free speed | `stroke`, `F_ext`, `F_ret`, `v_ext`, `v_ret` |
| end stops | 2e5 N/m with 300 N·s/m cushions | `k_stop`, `b_stop` |
| rod | 60 g moving mass, 5e4 N/m lateral and 200 N·m/rad bending stiffness in its guide | `m_rod`, `kt_rod`, `kr_rod` |
| cup | 1.5-fold bellows, lip 32 mm, A_eff 5 cm², L0 30 mm, axial 2500 N/m, lateral 1500 N/m, tilt 1 N·m/rad, µ 0.5 | `D_lip`, `A_eff`, `L0`, `k_ax`, `k_lat`, `k_tilt`, `mu` |
| ejector | one per zone, 60 kPa end vacuum, 12 l/min free air | `dp_max`, `Q_max` |
| carton | permeance 4e-7 m/(s·Pa) (cardboard) | `perm` |

**The cylinder** ([Cyl1](block:Cyl1), `library/PneumaticCylinder.jl`) is a force law on the rod joint's drive port. Its force is

$$F = \tfrac{1}{2}(1+u)F_{ext} - \tfrac{1}{2}(1-u)F_{ret} - b(u)\,v, \qquad b(u) = \tfrac{1}{2}(1+u)\frac{F_{ext}}{v_{ext}} + \tfrac{1}{2}(1-u)\frac{F_{ret}}{v_{ret}}.$$

The meter-out flow control acts as a damper that sets the free speed. A rod at its end stop presses with the full force. The port is power-in, so the rod speed is $v = -f$.

**Why one ejector per zone.** A zone whose cup leaks or peels does not pull down the vacuum of the others. With a common ejector, the leak of the worst cup loads all of them. The zones can also be switched off one by one (`zone1`..`zone5`), so a smaller parcel uses only the cups that land on it.

## Vacuum

The chamber of each cup is isothermal, with free-air flows. The vacuum level is $\Delta p$.

$$\dot p = \frac{p_{atm}}{V}\left(Q_{leak} + Q_{por} - Q_{gen}\right), \qquad Q_{gen} = Q_{max}\left(1 - \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta p_{max}}\right),$$

The steady state is the operating vacuum. There the ejector curve meets the lip leak plus the Darcy flow through the cardboard: **55.83 kPa** (`reference.py`; the simulation reads 55.83 kPa). From the lip contact the vacuum rises with a time constant of about 24 ms. In the default run it is at 55.76 kPa at the lift.

## The holding margin

`SuctionCup` reports the utilisation of each cup:

$$U = \frac{T + 4 M_{tilt}/D_{lip} + F_{lat}/\mu}{A_{eff}\,\Delta p}.$$

$T$ is the axial pull, $M_{tilt}$ the bending moment carried by the bellows and $F_{lat}$ the shear. At $U = 1$ the lip pulls off, peels or slips. [Monitor](block:Monitor) samples every cup each millisecond while the carton is carried. It keeps the peak $U_k$ of every active zone and the margin $1 - U_k$, with `M_tot` the smallest. The requirement `M_req` is 0.5, a safety factor of 2 (0.3 for the loose contents). A drop is the carton centre moving more than `d_drop` = 20 mm from where it sat in the gripper at the lift. It latches `held_ok` to 0.

# Optimisation and results

`design/optimize.py` searches each load case and family for the shortest cycle time that satisfies three constraints:

- the holding margin `M_tot ≥ M_req`, with no drop, in the reduced model;
- every robot limit at 100 % or below (`planner.reference`);
- the carton bottom at least 20 mm above the support, except within 50 mm (horizontally) of the pick and the place.

**Decision variables.** A speed scale `s` and the shape of the trajectory:
- the acceleration-to-speed ratio `rho`;
- the jerk share `lam`;
- the lift height `H_lift`;
- the blend distance `r_b` (families 3 and 4);
- the vacuum wait `T_seal`.

For joint moves `kv = s` and `ka = rho s`. For Cartesian moves the speeds are `s` times 2.5 m/s and 6 rad/s, and the accelerations and jerks are `rho s` times 20 m/s², 40 rad/s², 400 m/s³ and 800 rad/s³.

**Search.** For one shape, T_cyc falls as `s` grows, and so does the margin. A bisection therefore finds the fastest feasible `s`, first on the robot limits (the planner alone) and then on the margin. The shapes are searched on a grid of two values per variable, followed by three passes of a coordinate search.

**The reduced model.** `design/reduced.py` uses the bg-sim laws of `SuctionCup`, `ContactPoint` and the content walls. It holds the rods rigid at their extended stop and uses the same planner output. Compiled with numba, a run takes about 0.5 s (6 s with the loose ball).

| Load case | Default (family 3) | Family 1 joint PTP | Family 2 Cartesian | Family 3 Cartesian blended | Family 4 joint blended | Best |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Carton 1.5 kg, centred CoM | 1.659 s, M -0.07 | 1.950 s (M 0.50, robot 57 %) | 2.071 s (M 0.50, robot 68 %) | 1.635 s (M 0.50, robot 68 %) | 1.578 s (M 0.50, robot 57 %) | **1.578 s** (family 4) |
| Carton 1.5 kg, CoM offset (60, 30) mm, gripped over the CoM | 1.683 s, M -0.08 | 1.956 s (M 0.51, robot 55 %) | 2.097 s (M 0.50, robot 66 %) | 1.644 s (M 0.50, robot 66 %) | 1.597 s (M 0.51, robot 55 %) | **1.597 s** (family 4) |
| Carton 1.5 kg, CoM offset (60, 30) mm, gripped at the box centre | 1.659 s, M -4.05 (drop) | infeasible | infeasible | infeasible | infeasible | none: margin 0.19 at 5 % speed (needs 0.5) |
| Carton 0.6 kg + loose ball 0.5 kg | 1.659 s, M +0.09 | 1.955 s (M 0.33, robot 57 %) | 1.751 s (M 0.30, robot 68 %) | 1.392 s (M 0.30, robot 68 %) | 1.482 s (M 0.31, robot 57 %) | **1.392 s** (family 3) |
| Carton 0.6 kg + loose bottle 0.5 kg | 1.659 s, M +0.05 | 1.799 s (M 0.33, robot 57 %) | 1.721 s (M 0.30, robot 68 %) | 1.359 s (M 0.31, robot 68 %) | 1.461 s (M 0.30, robot 57 %) | **1.359 s** (family 3) |

What the optimiser finds:
- **The gripper sets the cycle time, not the robot.** Every optimum uses only 55–68 % of the
  LR-10iA/10 limits. The peak gripper acceleration while carrying is 8.4–9.8 m/s², about 1 g. Going
  faster is possible for the arm (`too-aggressive`: 1.36 s at 83 % of the limits) but not for the cups.
- **The default trajectory is not safe.** Family 3 at its default limits takes 1.659 s, but its margin
  is −0.07: the most loaded cup goes past its capacity during the transfer. The carton just stays on
  (bg-sim and the reduced model agree). The optimum, family 4 (joint blended), takes 1.578 s with a margin
  of 0.50: 5 % faster and safe.
- **Blending is what pays.** The families that stop at the corners take 1.95 s (joint PTP) and 2.07 s
  (Cartesian). Blending cuts 0.34–0.47 s. For the solid carton the joint blend is fastest (1.578 s against
  1.635 s for the Cartesian blend). For the loose contents the Cartesian blend is fastest (by 0.09–0.10 s).
- **No vacuum wait.** Every optimum has `T_seal` = 0: the vacuum is ready within the time the
  cylinders and the approach take. The Cartesian optima lift only 30 mm; the joint-blended optima lift
  70–80 mm with an 80 mm blend, so the transfer starts while the carton still rises.
- **Grip over the centre of mass.** With the CoM 60/30 mm off centre, gripping over the CoM costs only
  1 % (1.597 s). Gripping at the box centre is infeasible at any speed: at 5 % speed the margin is 0.19.
- **Loose contents.** The 1.1 kg load with M_req 0.3 can go faster (1.39 s with the ball, 1.36 s with the
  bottle). The content's impacts on the walls set the margin.

# Notes and limitations

- **Arm.** The geometry is that of the LR Mate 200iD/7L, with the limits of the LR-10iA/10. Its acceleration and jerk limits are assumed (see Robot and trajectories). The arm is kinematic: the flange follows the plan exactly. A real controller adds servo lag and path deviation in blends that are not modelled here.
- **Cups and air.** The cups are lumped `SuctionCup` elements. There are no hose dynamics: the ejector acts directly on the cup chamber. The cylinders have no air dynamics either: the valve sets the force at once and the meter-out damper sets the speed.
- **Reduced model.** It holds the rods rigid, while bg-sim keeps them compliant (about 145 Hz laterally). The margins agree within a few hundredths (see Validation and checks).
- **Solver.** The models use `Rodas5P`. With FBDF the run stops when the cup lips land: the lip gap then falls through the `h_lost` level of the cup's `lost` latch, and FBDF forces its step below machine precision.
- **Contents.** The loose ball and bottle are rigid bodies with Hunt–Crossley wall contacts and regularised Coulomb friction. They have no rolling resistance, so the ball keeps rolling.
- **Regenerating.** See the README: `build_library.py`, `build_scene.py`, `optimize.py` (about 25 min on 20 cores), `build_models.py`, `reference.py` and `build_expected.py`.

# Offset centre of mass, gripped at the box centre

The carton of `pick-place` with its centre of mass 60 mm along its length and 30 mm across (`cx`, `cy`), gripped at the box centre, on the default trajectory.

The offset makes a moment of $m g c$ = 0.88 N·m (x) and 0.44 N·m (y) about the grip point. The cups on the heavy side pull harder. The soft bellows let the carton tilt by about 4°, and the tilt moment adds to the peel load ($4M/D_{lip}$). Already at rest the most loaded cup is at U = 0.65 (see `static-hold`), so no trajectory reaches the margin of 0.5 with this grip: `optimize.py` finds no feasible speed for any family. On the default trajectory the heavy-side cups peel during the lift, and the carton drops.

**Gripping over the centre of mass** (`gx`, `gy` = `cx`, `cy`) removes the static moment. Then the offset case is almost as fast as the centred carton: see `offset-com-best`.

# Static hold: the cup forces against a hand calculation

The offset-CoM carton (60 mm, 30 mm), gripped at the box centre, carried slowly: Cartesian with stops at 0.1 m/s. In the cruise of the transfer, the carton hangs still. There the cup forces can be checked against a hand calculation (`design/reference.py`).

The carton is a rigid plate on five bellows. Each bellows has an axial spring $k_{ax}$ in series with the cylinder end stop, giving $k_s$, and a tilt spring $k_{tilt}$ in series with the rod bending, giving $k_t$. The centre of mass hangs $d = H_p/2$ below the lip plane, which adds a pendulum stiffness $m g d$:

$$K = 2 k_s a^2 + 5 k_t + m g d, \qquad \varphi_x = \frac{m g c_x}{K}, \quad \varphi_y = \frac{m g c_y}{K}, \qquad T_i = \frac{m g}{5} + k_s\,(x_i \varphi_x + y_i \varphi_y).$$

| zone | hand [N] | simulation at t = 6.52 s [N] |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 (centre) | 2.943 | 2.943 |
| 2 (+x) | 9.281 | 9.274 |
| 3 (+y) | 6.112 | 6.100 |
| 4 (−x) | −3.395 | −3.387 |
| 5 (−y) | −0.226 | −0.213 |

The sum is $m g$ = 14.715 N. Negative forces push: the light side presses its bellows. The vacuum is at the operating point, 55.83 kPa (hand calculation 55.83 kPa). The carton tilts by $\varphi_x$ = 3.7°. With the bellows moment, the most loaded cup reaches U = 0.65 at rest. That is why the centre grip cannot hold this carton with a margin of 0.5 (see `offset-com`).

# A loose ball in the carton

A 0.6 kg carton shell with a loose 0.5 kg ball (80 mm), on the default trajectory (family 3). The ball is a second rigid body. [Walls](block:Walls) (`library/SphereInBox.jl`, generated) gives it a compliant contact with the six inner walls. Each wall has a Hertz spring with Hunt–Crossley damping and regularised Coulomb friction, unrolled per wall, with no events. The carton is drawn see-through (diffuse alpha 0.35) so the ball shows.

When the gripper accelerates, the carton moves and the ball lags. It rolls to the trailing wall, hits it, and rolls back at the deceleration. Each impact is a short force peak on the carton (up to 150 N), which the cups have to carry. The soft bellows also let the carton tilt a little, so the ball keeps rolling and ends against a wall. That is a static shift of the centre of mass, like `offset-com`. Even at a crawl, the most loaded cup reaches U ≈ 0.6, so for the loose contents the optimiser asks a margin of 0.3 (safety factor 1.4) instead of 0.5. On this default trajectory the margin is 0.09: it holds, without reserve.
