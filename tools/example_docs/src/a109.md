# A109 open loop

The Agusta A109 of Heffley and Mnich's *Minimum-Complexity Helicopter Simulation Math Model* (NASA CR-177476, 1988, Appendix D). It is built as a bond graph:

* a multibond rigid body;
* one modulated wrench source per component (main rotor, fuselage, tail rotor, tailplane, fin);
* a rotational bond graph for the drivetrain.

Four helicopters are released from trim at sea level:

* **Hover**: the trim, released.
* **Coll**: a +0.5 deg collective step in hover.
* **Lon**: a 0.5 deg forward cyclic pulse in hover.
* **Fwd**: a 1 deg pedal pulse at 100 kt.

![The model canvas|900](@fig:diagram)

| block | role |
| --- | --- |
| [Hover](block:Hover), [Coll](block:Coll), [Lon](block:Lon), [Fwd](block:Fwd) | one airframe each, an instance of the library subsystem `HeliAirframe`: rigid body, air data, the five components, drivetrain and governor |
| [Hoverth0](block:Hoverth0), [CollB1](block:CollB1) and the like | the trim blade angles (deg) and the steps and pulses |
| [HoverView](block:HoverView) and the like | `HeliView`: keeps Hover framed; rotor disc and tail rotor poses |

## What to try

1. Press **Run**. Coll climbs and its nose yaws right. The main-rotor torque grows with the collective, and its reaction turns the fuselage: the collective→yaw coupling comes out of the drivetrain bond graph.
2. Watch Lon. The cyclic pulse starts the hover phugoid, an oscillation of about 10 s period that grows ($+0.21 \pm 0.60j$ 1/s).
3. Switch the dashboard's rotor speed to the report's constant 385 rpm and run again. The trims stay the same, but Coll yaws further, because the rotor no longer droops.

## 3D view

Hover (red) is framed. Coll (white) flies 16 m to its right, Lon (blue) to its left and Fwd (yellow) behind; Fwd is shown relative to its 100 kt trimmed path. The rotor discs tilt with the flapping. The blades spin at a tenth of their real speed.

# Structure

## Rigid body and components

The report's mass is 5401 lb (2450 kg), with $I_x = 1300$, $I_y = 6760$, $I_z = 6407$ and $I_{xz} = 800$ slug ft². The product of inertia tilts the principal axes by 10.49 deg about the body y axis, so the `RigidBody3D` sits on them.

Each component's force is a modulated source: an `ExternalWrenchSpatial` bonded through its own `FixedTransform3DNeutral` at the component's point (hub, fuselage, tail-rotor hub, tailplane, fin). The bond graph therefore forms $r \times F$. The component blocks compute the report's equations in SI:

* `MainRotor`: first-order tip-path-plane flapping and the thrust/inflow balance, eight unrolled Newton steps. It also applies the A109 revisions:
  * decoupled flapping;
  * the low-speed magnification of the dihedral effect below 30 kt.
* `Fuselage`: the drag areas, with the downwash centre of pressure magnified 3x.
* `TailRotor`: the tail rotor's thrust and inflow.
* `Tailplane` and `Fin`: the wake edge on the tailplane, the downwash factor, and stall at $|w_a| > 0.3|u|$.

## Drivetrain (ours)

The report holds the rotor speed constant. Here the rotor speed is a bond-graph degree of freedom:

* **Rotor 1-junction.** The rotor inertia $J$ = 1265 kg m², the aerodynamic torque $Q = P/\Omega$ of the report's power method (`RotorPower`) as a modulated effort source, and a flow sensor.
* **Mast 0-junction.** `ShaftAxis` couples the mast to the airframe's spatial port. The rotor's absolute speed is $\Omega_{rel} + k \cdot \omega$, and the mast torque acts back on the airframe.
* **Gearbox 1-junction.** The engine source driven by the `Governor` (a PI, $\omega_n$ = 2.5 rad/s, $\zeta$ = 0.8, at most 740 shp), the 90 hp transmission loss, and a TF ($n$ = 5.40) to the tail-rotor junction with the tail-rotor torque.

The global `rpm_mode` sets the speed the rotor aerodynamics see: the drivetrain's (1) or the nominal 385 rpm (0, the report's model).

Flapping, inflow and air data are signal blocks: the report's flapping is a first-order lag without blade inertia, so it stores no energy.

# Results

The bg-sim runs match an independent scipy model of the same blocks (`design/heli_si.py`, LSODA 1e-10) to about 1e-5 in every state over the 10 s of the checks.

Collective step in hover:

![Collective step|900](@fig:coll)

The thrust rises, the helicopter climbs (13 m after 10 s) and the torque reaction yaws it right (34 deg/s). The rotor droops 0.25 % and the governor brings it back.

The hover phugoid after the cyclic pulse:

![Hover phugoid|900](@fig:phugoid)

The report publishes no eigenvalues. The scipy linearisation gives $+0.211 \pm 0.598j$ (longitudinal) and $+0.051 \pm 0.620j$ (lateral) in hover. The check fits a damped oscillation to the pitch attitude of Lon and reads $\zeta = -0.28$, $\omega_n$ = 0.66 rad/s, against −0.33 and 0.63 rad/s.

The pedal pulse at 100 kt:

![Pedal pulse at 100 kt|900](@fig:fwd)

With the constant-RPM switch (`rpm_mode = 0`) the collective step yaws further, 189 deg of heading at 10 s against 170 deg:

![Constant rotor speed|900](@fig:rpm)

The trims against the report's fitted model (power, attitudes and the four control positions from hover to 120 kt and in sideward flight) are tabulated in the example's README. Power, pitch, collective, pedals and longitudinal cyclic follow the report's curves. The roll attitude and the lateral cyclic in forward flight do not; the README flags this.

# The flyable A109

The A109 of the open-loop model with ground effect at 15 m, flown with collective, cyclic and pedals:

* `Rigging` maps the sticks to the blade angles.
* A switchable SCAS holds the attitude and the heading. It fades out with the weight on the wheels.
* The helicopter stands on a tricycle gear: three stdlib `ContactPoint`s on a flat ground.
* A demonstration pilot flies 120 s:
  * hover;
  * accelerate and climb to 75 kt and about 45 m;
  * a coordinated 20 deg bank turn through 146 deg;
  * decelerate and hover.

The sticks, the wind and the SCAS switch pass through export markers: in bg-sim they carry the demonstration pilot, in an FMU the host's pilot drives them. The start state (pose, momenta, flapping, rotor momentum, governor) is a set of globals, so an FMU host can start on the pad or in any trim. The browser game Island Lift (`apps/web` in the repository) flies the exported FMU in real time.

| --- | --- |
| [Heli](block:Heli) | the airframe (as in `a109-open-loop`), with wind inputs, ground effect, smoothed switches and the landing gear: `Tyre_nose`, `Tyre_left`, `Tyre_right` (`ContactPoint`) on `Ground` (`ContactSurface`), the weight on wheels `Wow` |
| [Pilot](block:Pilot) | `DemoPilot`: speed by pitch attitude, height by collective, the turn by bank |
| [UCmd](block:UCmd), [HCmd](block:HCmd), [PhTurn](block:PhTurn) | the schedule over time (`Lookup1D`) |

| [ScasSw](block:ScasSw), [scas](block:scas) | the SCAS switch (the global `scas`) through an `ExportInputPass` |

## What to try

1. Press **Run** and watch the demonstration in 3D.
2. Set **demo** to 0. The helicopter hovers hands-off. Add a longitudinal offset of 0.1: the SCAS holds the commanded 1.5 deg nose-down attitude and the helicopter accelerates gently.
3. Then set **SCAS** to 0. The bare helicopter's hover phugoid diverges within 10 s.

# Results

The demonstration:

![Demonstration|900](@fig:demo)

The scipy closed loop of `design/heli_sim.py` flies the same demonstration; bg-sim follows it to about 1e-7 over the 120 s. The rotor speed stays within 0.15 % of nominal. The power runs from 640 hp in hover down to about 430 hp at 75 kt, well inside the 740 shp take-off power.

Hands-off with the SCAS on, 0.1 forward stick from 1 s:

![Attitude hold|900](@fig:hold)

With the SCAS off, 0.02 forward stick:

![Bare helicopter|900](@fig:bare)

From the pad, collective 3 % above the hover's: the wheels unload within half a second, the SCAS engages and holds the hover attitude, and the helicopter climbs.

![Lift-off from the gear|900](@fig:liftoff)

Parked with the collective down, it rests level on its three wheels at a CG height of 1.28 m (4 cm of tyre deflection), and the rotor stays at 100 %.

The closed loop runs with the report's hard switches smoothed (`smooth` = 1, ours). The fin-stall switch between 40 and 55 kt otherwise traps the trajectory and the closed loop chatters. The tailplane's downwash factor is a smooth max, not a condition: a fixed-step FMU holds a condition over its step, and where the descent velocity passes the induced velocity the wake-edge distance swings through ±10⁴ m within one step.
